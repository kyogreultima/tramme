# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).
Until 1.0.0, the document format and the APIs may still change between minor versions.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **A timeline by tracks** ([montage.md](docs/montage.md)): a composition can be a *sequence*, its clips on picture and sound tracks with lock, hide, mute, solo, a magnetic mode that closes gaps, a height, and an adjustment clip for a range of tracks. The format gains optional fields only (`kind: "sequence"`, `tracks`, a clip's `track` and `link`, a track's `volume`, `pan`, `bus`, `to`); a document without them is read as before.
- **The edits of a video editor**, one function each in `@tramme/core` and the same for the keys, the mouse, the assistant: ripple delete, ripple trim, roll, slip, slide, insert, overwrite, reorder on a magnetic track, linked clips, a video's sound detached on its own track, copy and paste with the look, cut at the playhead (Ctrl+B, Q, W, Shift+Delete, L, `,` and `.`).
- **Media**: bins, tags and a search that also finds a word said in a clip; a source monitor with in and out marks and **sub-clips**; media gone missing listed offline (the preview goes on, an export names them, "Find the files…" brings them back); clips lined up **by their sound**; **multicam** (one camera per track, a panel that cuts between them); drag a media or a composition onto a lane; several compositions open in tabs.
- **Sound**: a mixer with a strip per sound track and bus (volume, pan, mute, solo, automation written by moving a fader while the film plays), a loudness target and a measure, and a **voice-over recorded** on the timeline.
- **Picture**: **match the look** of a shot to another's with editable colour wheels; **Real-ESRGAN** as a choice for enlarging pictures (the built-in Lanczos stays the default).
- **Publishing**: presets for a vertical short, a square, a landscape film, a 4K master and a GIF, with what stands in the way listed and fixed; **export the marked part** (I and O); **exports in line**, each on the film as it was asked for; **chapters** (markers of kind `chapter`) written into the files the command line renders, and copied as a list from the browser.
- **The assistant** has the editing operations as tools: `ripple_delete`, `insert_clip`, `overwrite_clip`, `ripple_trim`, `roll`, `slip`, `slide`, `detach_audio`, `link`, `sync_clips`, `add_adjustment`, `match_look`, `publish`. A guided tour of the lot.
- **The hesitations cut by the fillers tool can be reviewed and put back one by one** from the media panel.
- **Guided tours for what had none**: *Grade and match a look* (the scopes, matching a shot to another, the 10-bit export) *Make it yours* (tokens, plugins and packs, the tours plugins bring) and *The tools of the / menu* (footage, voice, the material read, whole motion pieces, following an object, pictures and clips by a provider, reuse, checks). The tricks tour gains masks and the viewport's safe zones, grid and onion skin; the home tour names the language setting. The montage tour gains several cameras, adjustment clips and chapters; the sound tour, recording a voice-over; the assistant tour, dictation and the messages that wait while it works. A test checks that every place a tour points at exists in the interface and is listed in [docs/tours.md](docs/tours.md).

### Fixed

- **The atelier's tour is offered when a project first goes to the atelier**, not only when one opens in it: the studio is the default view, so the tour was reached only through the help menu. The editor tour's last step now lists the tours that exist.
- **A tour step whose place is missing no longer costs the next step its preparation**: the step is skipped, but the next one still opens its tab. The mixer step of the montage tour points at the tabs until the film has a sound track, instead of vanishing.
- **The voice detector loaded the wrong onnxruntime build** (the one whose wasm files transformers.js does not configure), so it fell back on the energy of the sound; it now loads the build the other models use.
- **The editor's `time` helpers moved to `@tramme/core`** (`packages/core/src/timing.ts`), the editor re-exporting them: the montage operations and the assistant need them without the interface.

## [0.15.1] - 2026-10-28

### Fixed

- **The local companion transcribes in the language spoken too**: it ran Whisper without a language, so transformers.js took English. It now reads the language from Whisper's first step, as the browser does, and tells it back so the next chunks of a long sound keep it.
- **A video added from the assets plays whole**: its layer is as long as its file, and a composition shorter than it lengthens to hold it (one undo takes both back). A 12-minute video added to a new 5-second project was cut to 5 seconds and looped there.
- **The cut in the file reaches past the composition**: stretching a video's end past the composition's end lengthens the composition with it, so what was taken is seen and played instead of falling after the end.
- **The model chosen stays chosen**: the companion missing one of the editor's checks (busy with a turn or a transcription) no longer replaces the model picked with another connected AI's; that takeover now only happens at startup, while the chosen model has never answered.
- **A media file's length that cannot be read no longer hangs**: reading it gives up after two retries, where the media library's default tried again for ever.

## [0.15.0] - 2026-10-28

### Added

- **A queue for the messages written while the assistant works**: every message box (the conversation, the Studio's direction bar, the notes) keeps accepting messages during a turn. They wait above the box, each with a button to take it back, and leave together when the turn ends, as one turn of the assistant, each with the context of the moment it was written (the time, the selection, the scene, the pin), so "this layer" is the layer that was selected then. After a stop or a failed turn the queue stays, with a "Send now" button, and leaves with the next message; a turn that ends with a proposal waiting for a decision holds it until the proposal is applied or rejected. The queue survives a reload of the page.
- **Dictation**: a microphone in every message box (the home page, the conversation, the Studio's direction bar, the notes). Click to speak, click to stop, and what you said is written where the cursor is, for you to read and send; Escape drops the recording. It runs on the downloaded Whisper model first (Whisper base, 80 MB, is offered when none is downloaded), and a setting in Models, Sound chooses the downloaded model, the companion or the server. The desktop app asks for the microphone (`NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` on macOS).
- **Haiku 5.5** in the model list, next to Haiku 4.5, with adaptive thinking and the five effort levels (no server-side fallback is asked for it). The default model does not change.
- **The conversation's cache lifetime, as a setting**: 5 minutes (as before) or an hour, in Settings, Behavior. An hour costs more to write and less when you come back after more than five minutes.
- **The bench prices and compares the assistant's turns**: every route now reports its usage (the chat-format providers through `stream_options`, with the cached share where each gives it; cache writes split by lifetime; each turn names its route, and says when it has no usage rather than counting zero). `node bench/usage.ts` adds the dollars (a price table for Claude, "unknown" for a model without one, "at least" for Haiku 5.5), the cache share and the route per turn, per conversation and in all; `node bench/usage.ts --compare <before> <after>` compares two series of runs. Costs stay in the bench, never in the chat.
- **Clips, made by a provider** ([generation-roadmap.md](docs/generation-roadmap.md), step 5): `generate-video` has Veo 3.1 Fast, through Hydracept, make a 4, 6 or 8 second shot from words, or animate a still picture of the project as its opening frame. The clip is saved under `assets/videos/` with its record (`.video.json`), proposed as a video layer at the playhead, and answered with a frame so the assistant judges it; `again` and `change` make it again from its record. A new Videos section in the settings sets the provider and the length. Each second is billed, so the user says yes first, as for the pictures and the sounds.

### Changed

- **The system prompt is in cache order**: the same text, reordered, so that the reference (`docs/document.md`) follows the instructions and comes before what changes with the project (the installed plugins, the packs, the brief). The Messages API caches the fixed part apart for an hour, so it is served from the cache across projects; the other routes get the same order in one piece.

### Fixed

- **The local companion is found whenever it starts**: the app looks for it again every few seconds while it is missing and every fifteen once it answers, on every screen and whichever route works, and when the window regains focus. A companion started after the app, or one that stops, is noticed without a reload.
- **Speech in any language, transcribed in it**: transformers.js transcribes as English when it is not told the language, so a French dictation came back as English words. Whisper's own first step now tells the language spoken, for dictations and for the sounds transcribed in the browser.
- **Dictation, quicker the first time**: it runs Whisper on the processor in the compact weights the download offer names (77 MB), where a GPU fetched the full ones (291 MB), and shows the download as a percentage beside the microphone. A second click while the microphone opens no longer opens it twice, and the recording time never shows below zero.

## [0.14.0] - 2026-10-26

### Added

- **Shorts, framed closer and cut from any video** ([shorts-roadmap.md](docs/shorts-roadmap.md), steps 2 and 5): a speaker small in the frame gets a gentle push-in that settles over the short, and when the people detector hesitates the frame follows the cutout's mask instead. A video nobody speaks in — a match, a ride, a build-up — gets its candidates from its sound and its cuts (the build-ups into a louder section, the loudest moments, snapped to cuts and bar lines), described in words so the assistant picks the same way, and its shorts build without captions.
- **Pictures, chosen and changed** ([generation-roadmap.md](docs/generation-roadmap.md), steps 2 to 4): a new Pictures section in the settings sets the provider by default, the quality and the size at most (small asks the provider's cheapest size). `generate-image` makes up to four takes of an ask — a contact sheet in the conversation, one click keeps one, the others are never saved; makes a picture again from its record changing only what is asked (`again` and `change`); changes a reference — an image asset or a frame of the video — with the project's palette sent along (OpenAI and Gemini), a mask saying where (the cutout tool makes them), or extends it past its edges to a wider format; and brings a small picture to the size of the frame before placing it.
- **Logos in 3D** (`plugins/extrude.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 17): a logo that turns for an opening or an end card. The `extrude` tool (`/extrude`) takes the path layers of a logo or an icon (an SVG through svg-paths, or one path), reads its holes, extrudes it with a bevel and writes it as a `.glb` — one material per colour of the logo — that the built-in 3D model layer draws turning on its turntable under the studio light, its yaw, pitch and explode keyable. The faces are triangulated by ear clipping with the holes bridged in; the flat logo stays, hidden, to edit and extrude again. Type is extruded once it is outlined in the SVG.
- **Kinetic type, further** (`plugins/kinetic.js` 1.1.0, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 16): two new moves for a word, a name or a figure. `scramble` runs every character through others, seeded, before each settles in turn from left to right — a code breaking, a name revealed. `flap` lays the text on a split-flap board: each character flips through the drum to its own, the top half of the old flap folding down and the bottom half of the new one opening, a departures board or a counter. Both are pure functions of time, with a preset each.
- **QR codes** (`plugins/qr.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 15): an end card the viewer scans. The pack encodes the code itself (ISO/IEC 18004: byte mode with UTF-8, versions 1 to 20, the four correction levels, the mask of least penalty) — its symbols are the same, module for module, as a reference encoder's. The `qr.code` node draws the modules square, rounded or as dots in ink on a plate with its quiet zone, a logo in the middle (the code goes to level H to carry it), revealed module by module as a sweep or a seeded scatter, and exports as SVG and Lottie. The `qr-scannable` check flags modules under 5 px, a quiet zone under four modules, light modules on a dark plate or ink under 4:1, and a code on screen for less than 3 s.
- **Print looks** (`plugins/print.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 14): a whole film printed as a risograph. Four GLSL finishing passes, computed on the frame in linear light inside the pass: `print.halftone` (an AM screen of one ink on a grid turned by the angle — the cell sets how coarse it reads), `print.riso` (two inks, each a little off register, multiplied into paper that carries a grain), `print.grain` (film grain sized by the luma — brighter places carry bigger grains), `print.duotone` (the picture's light laid between two colours). Three stacked presets: risograph, newsprint, two-tone. Each pass compiles in the render tests' Chrome and gives its known pixels on a gradient.
- **Rigid bodies** (`plugins/physics.js` 1.1.0, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 13): a small engine in the pack — boxes, circles and convex polygons falling, colliding, stacking and coming to rest. `stepWorld` is pure (frame 90 is the same reached directly or frame by frame), solves with sequential impulses over SAT contacts (the least-overlap axis, the contact clipped to the face, a twin point when a face meets a face — a stack needs two to stand), friction in the cone, restitution fading under a walking pace, a touch of damping, the overlap eased out and a body slow for a while put to sleep. The `phys.bodies` node runs it through the engine's checkpoints with the frame's sides and floor as static bodies; the text drop's letters are bodies now, so they knock into each other and pile up the way they land.
- **Matter pack** (`plugins/matter.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 12): the row the plugins roadmap left open. The `matter.morph` node walks both shapes again at equal arc length and matches their winding and their starts (the rotation of the second that meets the first with the least summed distance), so an icon becomes another without twisting, its curves followed, several contours paired by size and place and the extra ones growing from a point. The `matter.liquid` node paints seeded blobs that merge and part — a field of circles traced by marching squares, oriented so the loops chain, drifting as slow as lava, or as a flood crossing the frame from the left and draining back. The `matter.paper` node sweeps a sheet across the frame, covering it at the cut, its edge torn by seeded noise with its fibres and its shadow, the same in every render. All pure functions of t: the liquid answers any frame in any order.
- **Formulas** (`plugins/math.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 11): a TeX subset laid out by the pack in STIX Two Math (OFL) — no MathJax, no network. The parser reads fractions, scripts, roots, sums and integrals with their limits, small matrices (`\begin{pmatrix}`), `\text`, `\color` and `\mathbb`, the Greek letters and the common operators (`\cdot \times \le \ge \ne \infty \to`), an unknown command throwing with its position; the layout walks the tree and places each glyph from the font's own table (`glyphs.json`, 216 glyphs extracted once from the font by `scripts/packs/math_glyphs.py` + `scripts/packs/math.ts` through the SVG reader), each symbol an id stable per occurrence so the `math.formula` node can change one formula into another — the shared symbols move to their places, the others fade and arrive, the way a proof unfolds on a board. Variables are set in the font's math italic. The `formula` tool copies the glyph table into the project once, so a formula draws the same everywhere, and refuses a symbol the font does not carry; the `formula-legibility` check flags a size under the format's floor and a formula on screen for less than its reading time (slower than text).
- **Audiogram pack** (`plugins/audiogram.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 10): a podcast extract as a clip for the feeds. `analyseAudio` now computes the sound's 32 log bands (40 Hz to 16 kHz, an FFT of 2048 samples at every frame of its 50 Hz rate, saved decimated to 12.5 Hz — a 30-minute analysis stays under 6 MB), and the `audiogram.wave` node draws them as bars, a mirrored wave, a line or a ring at the moment of the extract that plays, rising with the voice and falling back like a meter, a playhead crossing; the `audiogram` tool lays the clip out, varied by a seed — cover behind, the show's name, the wave, the words said as captions when the sound has its transcript, a progress bar trimming itself across, the sound (or a video's own) playing from the moment asked. Each band is read against its own loudest moment, so a pause reads as a pause. A 1 kHz tone lights its band; the bars read the bands linearly between the frames.
- **Maps pack** (`plugins/maps.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 9): "our offices in Lyon, Montreal and Dakar" becomes a map, offline. Natural Earth (public domain: countries at 1:110m and 1:50m, first-level regions, 7 000+ populated places) ships beside the pack, simplified and quantised to 1e-4° by `scripts/packs/maps.ts`; the `map` tool (`/map`) finds places by name with the accents folded and the aliases known ("NYC", "Bombay"), asks back when a name is ambiguous ("Springfield" is five), picks the projection and the framing from what is shown, and writes into the project only the shapes the map shows (a two-country atlas is about 2 % of the 50m file). The `map.view` node draws the countries from the tokens, the named ones in the accent, pins with their labels kept apart, routes as great circles drawn on; the camera (centre and zoom) animates, and with a route it flies from one place to the next on van Wijk and Nuij's smooth zoom — out in the middle, exactly on its end, never faster than the eye follows — while the route draws itself, then pulls out to the whole map. A still map exports as SVG. The `map-legibility` check flags highlighted countries too small at their zoom, labels with no room beside their pins and a zoom that doubles in under 0.6 s.
- **Charts, further** (`plugins/charts.js` 1.1.0, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 8): the `chart` tool reads a CSV (separators `,` `;` and tabs sniffed from the first line, quoted fields with doubled quotes kept whole, decimal commas read as numbers) and, with `kind: 'race'`, turns a table of periods by entries into the `chart.race` node, keyed to run over the composition — bars sliding to their new rank, values rolling, the period shown large behind, the leaders kept in view and the tail cut (12 racing at most in 9:16, 20 in 16:9, the cut named). The `chart.scatter` node draws two measures together, points travelling between periods with their trails, labels kept apart. The `chart.function` node draws a curve the tool sampled (the points come computed; the node never evaluates a string), with the pack's readable ticks, a dot travelling along it, a tangent and the area under it on demand. The `chart-readability` check flags colliding labels on a scatter and more than 12 entries racing in 9:16.
- **Chat and emoji packs** (`plugins/chat.js`, `plugins/emoji.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 7): the text-message stories of short videos. The `chat` tool (`/chat`) reads a script (`A: …`, a time in `[2s]` when a line lands), times it by reading (max(0.9, words/3.3 + 0.3) a line, a reply that takes thought pausing longer), and plays it as the `chat.thread` node — bubbles entering on the kit's curves from their sender's side, a typing indicator before each incoming message, the thread scrolling to keep the last message in view — in the walkthrough pack's phone in 16:9, full frame in 9:16. The `chat.notice` node drops notifications in and stacks them for the cold open. The `chat-reading` check flags a message pushed away before its reading time and a typing indicator under 0.4 s. The `emoji` pack ships the 300 most used emoji as Fluent Emoji Flat SVG (MIT, converted by `scripts/packs/emoji.ts`), the `emoji` tool places them as picture layers by name or character — only the files used are copied into the project, and they draw the same on every machine, never the system's emoji font; given a chat thread, it draws the thread's emoji as the same pictures, inside the bubbles.
- **Handmade pack** (`plugins/handmade.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 6): warmth for explainers. The `fx.boil` layer effect (GLSL) redraws the layer a little off every 2 or 3 frames, so a machine-drawn line trembles like one drawn by hand (the amount in px, held between changes); the `twos` modifier holds any value for n frames with a small seeded jitter at each change, for stop motion and cut-out paper; the `sketch.shape` node draws a rough line, arrow, rectangle, ellipse or bracket as two seeded passes that overshoot their corners (rough.js's idea, in the pack's own code), hachured fill included, revealing with `progress`; the `annotate` tool (`/annotate`) draws a circle, an underline, a box, a highlight, a strike or a bracket around a layer, on a beat after it lands, its keys riding the layer's position when it moves. The `sketchbook` kit brings the paper plate, the graphite ink and slower curves, and Caveat (OFL, latin subset) ships beside the pack as the handwritten face.
- **Screencast pack** (`plugins/screencast.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 5): a screen recording played as a product film, without a key placed by hand. The `screencast` tool samples the recording (one look every half second, a 30-minute recording capped at 1 800 — one a second, and said), measures where the picture changes on a 32 px grid, tells the whole-screen change of a scroll or a page load from the local one, and saves the regions of interest over time as a JSON asset. From them it keys the video layer's zoom — in where the action is (eased at 1.5× a second at most, held while the region stays, out before each page change, never over 2.5×, never showing past the recording's edge) as ordinary scale and position keys on the composition's clock, and marks a found click (a small region after a still stretch) with the walkthrough pack's cursor ripple. The still stretches are named for the edit — a speed-up or a cut, offered, never applied. The `screencast-motion` check flags a zoom faster than 1.5× a second and a focus that leaves the picture.
- **Rhythm pack** (`plugins/rhythm.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 4): checks only, what a motion designer catches in a film that is right frame by frame and wrong as a whole. `off-beat` flags a cut landing more than 2 frames from every beat of the music (the film's beats analysis) and names the beat — the bar line, at a section change — to move it to; `even-shots` flags four shots or more of nearly the same length (spread under 15 % of the mean): the film plods; `palette-drift` flags fills and strokes written outside the tokens and far from all of them (ΔE over 10, CIE76), or more than five colours on screen at once; `type-drift` flags more than two typefaces in one film and near-duplicate sizes on one frame (within 10 %). The `/review` workflow adds the pack when the film has music.
- **Code view pack** (`plugins/codeview.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 3): code on screen for developer tools and product films. The `codeview.code` node colours code by a lexer written in the pack (js, ts, py, rs, go, sh, json, yaml, sql, css, html; tokens to the kit's accents, moved away from the panel until they read at 4.5:1, comments at 55 %), with line numbers, highlighted lines (the others dimmed, the view scrolled to the first), typing at a seeded human rhythm (18 characters a second, a pause at each line's end, the caret blinking), and a change between two versions — the tokens both share slide to their place, the removed fade, the new ones type in (a longest-common-subsequence diff of tokens). The `codeview.terminal` node runs a session written as a script (`$ command`, `> output`, `...` with seconds): commands typed, output arriving in chunks with its ANSI colours mapped to the tokens, a spinner and progress bars (`[###] 2`) stepping with time. The `code-screen` tool (`/code-screen`) lays code or a session in the walkthrough pack's new `window` frame (a title bar and tabs, no address bar), at the format's legibility floor (24 px on a 1080-high frame, 28 in 9:16), a long file cut to what fits with the cut named (the highlighted lines kept where they are), and the `code-legibility` check flags small type, more lines than fit, and typing faster than reading. The walkthrough pack is at 1.1.0 for the window frame.
- **A project's older copy of a pack offered its update**: the Code section lists an "Update to {version}" beside a copy of a shipped pack older than the editor's, written in place with its version recorded, the way an installed plugin's copy already is.
- **The sound heard while scrubbing**: dragging the playhead plays a short window of the sound at each step (80 ms, at most every 60 ms, never the videos' own streamed sound), enough to hear the moment a cut or a hit lands; a setting turns it off (on by default).
- **The `/` menu remembers what is used**: the commands a user actually runs open the menu first (at most six kept on this machine), and the search is unchanged once something is typed.
- **The packs' commands on the home screen**: the direction box's `/` menu lists the shipped packs' tools after the built-in ones, before a project opens — a pack command a project does not hold yet waits in the editor's box until the project's packs attach. The list comes from an index generated from the packs themselves (`npm run packs:index`: `plugins/index.json` gains each pack's commands and their French, `docs/packs.md` the site's new Packs page), which CI checks cannot go stale.
- **The desktop app asks for attention when a question waits**: while you are in another window, a question of the assistant or the editor flashes the taskbar (Windows, Linux) or bounces the Dock icon (macOS) besides its notification, until the window is focused or the question goes; in front of the window, the message alone, as before.
- **The preview's playback clock is pinned by tests**: the wrap of a loop, the end without one, and a playback overtaken by another or by a pause are each tested, out of the preview itself; nothing changes in how it plays.
- **The model chosen on the home screen**: the direction box offers the model menu beside the format, so the first turn of a new project runs on the model picked there.
- **A turn paused**: the assistant at work can be paused before its next tool and resumed, from the pill over the film or the studio; stopped while paused, the tool that waited never starts (a picture or a sound costs money).
- **The turn under way, in the studio**: what the assistant does now and for how long, the model it runs on, the stage it is at among all of them on a bar, its last three steps (all of them on demand), pause and stop.
- **What a tool did, under its line**: each call says how long it took, why it failed (the first line of its error) or the tool's own notice; a sound or a picture names the provider and the model that made it. Conversations saved before read these lines back from their history.

### Changed

- **The app is named Tramme**: the editor, the desktop app, the site, the assistant and the documentation write the name with a capital. The command line (`tramme`), the packages (`@tramme/…`), the `.tramme` archives, the project files and the stored settings keep their names.

### Removed

- **Films from a description** (`@tramme/film`): the `film`, `film-guide` and `film-review` tools, the `/film` workflow, the film kits (midnight, daylight, poster, studio, terminal, soft, luxe, paper), `tramme film` on the command line and the fonts shipped for them. Its compiler gave every brief the same few layouts. Words written on the home screen now start the assistant directly: it directs the film with the editor's own vocabulary (compositions per scene, templates, recipes, packs, kits) and checks it with `check`. A project made by the film tool still opens: its scenes are ordinary compositions, its description an ordinary file.

### Fixed

- The PNG sequence export said, in French, "une image par tramme" instead of "par trame".
- **A folder holding 3D models imports**: bringing in sources with a .glb or .gltf among them failed as a whole, the project format refusing those files under assets/ (in the browser, on the server and in the desktop app alike); they are now kept as model assets the 3D model layer draws, a large .glb sent in parts like a video. The extrude tool's .glb is written the same way.
- **The packs' forms in French**: the fields of the packs' tools in the `/` menu (charts, stick figures, walkthrough, localize, icons, diagrams) carried English titles and hints in a French editor; every field is translated, and a test now keeps them so.

## [0.13.0] - 2026-10-25

### Added

- **Diagrams pack** (`plugins/diagram.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 2): flows, architectures, cycles and trees written as flowchart text (a Mermaid subset: `A[Label] --> B(Round)`, `C{Decision}`, `-->|label|`, `---`, `-.->`, `subgraph`; `LR`, `TB`, `RL` or `BT` to force a direction, left out to let the format pick it — left to right in 16:9, top to bottom in 9:16; errors name their line) or the same as JSON. The parser reads it; a layout written in the pack, pure and deterministic, places it — layered (ranks by longest path, crossings reduced by barycentres, coordinates balanced), a ring for cycles, a hub for stars — and the `diagram` tool (`/diagram`) expands it into ordinary layers: boxes (`shape.rect`, the kit's radius), labels (wrapped, sized to the format), edges (`shape.path` with their arrowheads, drawn on with the trim between their ends' arrivals), revealed in reading order with an uneven stagger, in one group; a `highlight` node dims the rest and lifts one route through the graph in the accent. The `diagram-legibility` check flags a label overflowing its box or set too small, an edge crossing a label, and more boxes than the format holds at once (about 12 in 16:9, 8 in 9:16).
- **Icons pack** (`plugins/icons.js`, [packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md), step 1): Lucide 1.61.0 (ISC, 2 166 icons with their tags) shipped beside the pack as `plugins/icons/lucide.json`, converted by `scripts/packs/icons.ts` through the SVG reader into path values on the 24-unit grid. The `icon` tool (`/icon`) finds pictograms by name, tag or a few words (ranked search with synonyms of the common words: "money" finds `banknote`, `wallet`, `coins`), lays one or several in a row, a grid or beside the lines of a list, in the film's colours (@ink, @accent or a colour), each a group named `icon:<name>` of path layers with round caps and joins, its stroke riding the group's scale, its contours drawing themselves with the trim, staggered unevenly; when the words are vague, the candidates come back as a contact sheet of named icons. The `icon-stroke` check warns when an icon's stroke is under 2 px at the composition's size and when one group mixes weights.
- **Packs, step 0** ([packs-roadmap.md](docs/packs-roadmap.md)): the ground the packs stand on, in Tramme itself. **Strokes that draw themselves**: `trimStart`, `trimEnd` and `trimOffset` (0 to 1, animatable) on `shape.rect`, `shape.ellipse` and `shape.path`, measured along the outline's length and drawn as a window of the stroke (a dash over it), by Lottie's rule (an end before the start swaps with it; a rectangle's outline starts on its right side under the top right corner, an ellipse's at the top, both clockwise), so a trim draws the same stretch in the preview, in Lottie and in SVG; exported to Lottie as trim paths (`tm`, the fill kept whole in its own group) and to SVG with `pathLength` and a dash; the `shape.path` note rewritten on them, and the map-journey recipe reveals its line with them instead of a matte. **SVG to paths**: an SVG reader in `packages/interop` (path data with arcs as cubic Béziers, circle, ellipse, rect, line, polyline, polygon, their transforms and their groups' presentation, one contour per shape; the viewBox mapped into the drawing's size, an unstyled shape filled in black as SVG does, arc flags read even when written against the next number) beside the export, and the built-in `svg-paths` tool (assistant and `/` menu) that turns an SVG asset into a group of path layers with their fills and strokes, its strokes drawing themselves on the kit's curves. **Packs that carry data**: a pack may hold a folder beside it (`plugins/<pack>/`), its files read at authoring time with `ctx.packFile` from the editor's own origin, offered to a plugin whose `meta.id` is a shipped pack's (`tramme/<pack>`, which the packs now declare) and to a project's own copy; the data is never copied into the project. **The assistant reaches the packs**: the shipped packs' nodes, tools and presets are found by `list_nodes { query }` even when the project holds none (counted, not listed), a proposal that draws with one adds it to the project in the same undo step (the installed plugins' path: the copy on first draw, the version recorded), and the system prompt names them in one line. **One test for every pack**: `packs.test.ts` reads `plugins/index.json` and runs the shared checks on each pack it lists — registers, every node validates with its default props, and every title, label and description carries its French.
- **Stick figures pack**: Pivot and StykAnimate-style characters in `plugins/styk.js`. The `styk.figure` node draws a rig of fixed-length segments (after StykAnimate's "Blue" and Pivot's default man, or any figure the project defines): the `pose` property gives each segment an angle relative to its parent, held key to key like Pivot animates, driven by an expression, or reaching a `{ target: [x, y] }` with two-bone IK (`bend` picks the elbow or knee side) and stretching with `{ a, l }`; keyframed poses hold, the way Pivot's frames do. The `styk-figure` tool lays a figure in the composition (the man, the plain black one, or one already walking — a 1 s walk cycle of eight held poses), and the `styk-figure` check flags a pose naming a segment the figure does not have, an IK target the limb cannot reach, two targets on one chain, and a rig with a missing parent, a cycle or a duplicate id.
- **The / menu on the home screen**: the direction box offers the tools and workflows as the message box does (the same menu, shared). A project started with `/name` sends its words with that command, as the message box would; a tool named alone waits in the editor's box with its form.
- **Native notifications in the desktop app**: a question waiting for the user's answer and the end of an assistant turn are told by the system's notifications (Tauri's notification plugin), the web view showing none of the web's.

### Fixed

- Playback could start again from where it was paused rather than where the playhead had been brought, and play every sound twice or more at once, right after Play or when a loop wrapped: until the sounds had loaded, the frames still read the last playback's clock (the audio context runs on while paused), which landed past the end, wrapped and started another playback over the first. ## [0.12.0] - 2026-10-21

### Added

- **Time displacement**: the `time.shift` node reads a composition later in some places than in others: slit-scan along a side, a time ripple from the centre or the edges, or a liquid time in noise patches. It keeps a ring of past frames as a 3D texture and stays a pure function of time.
- **Faster blurs, shadows and glows**: a layer's Canvas 2D filters now run over its box, grown by what they spread, instead of the whole frame. The `effects` load case renders in 6.8 s a frame instead of 27.4 s on a machine without GPU raster, with the same pixels. An effect can say how far its filter spreads (`canvas2d.reach`); without it, the whole frame is filtered as before.
- **Installed plugins in every project**: an installed plugin's tools, checks, workflows, kits and presets are offered in every project. They run in the project's sandbox, never on the page, and the project's own names win. A project can turn one off (`meta.pluginsOff`). When a layer, an effect or a modifier of one first enters a project, the plugin and its own files are copied into the project's `plugins/` in the same undo step. Its version is recorded in `meta.installedCopies`, so the project opens the same anywhere, and a newer installed version is offered per project, never applied without the user. A project holding such a copy is always drawn through the sandbox. A plugin reaches only what it declared (`meta.permissions`): its network origins and provider routes through the new `ctx.fetch`, and writes under `assets/` and its declared paths. It is stopped for the session after three failures in a row. The assistant's `list_nodes` takes a `query` that finds the installed plugins' nodes and tools, left out of its index, and the system prompt names the installed plugins.
- **Installed plugins**: a Plugins section in the settings installs a plugin once on this device, from a `.js` module or a `.trammeplugin` package (a zip of the module and its own files), picked or dropped. It asks first, before any of the plugin's code runs: what the plugin brings, what it may reach and its API version. An update asks again only when it asks for more. Each plugin can be turned off or removed, and all of them can be exported as one backup and imported back. The plugins are kept in the desktop app's folder, in a folder the user picks (Chrome and Edge, with the File System Access API), in the browser's storage (made persistent where the browser allows), or on the private deployment's server. A plugin's `meta` grows an `id`, a `licence`, an `author`, a `homepage` and its `permissions`; plugins without them stay valid.
- **10-bit exports**: `tramme render --depth 10` reads each frame at 16 bits from the compositor's half-float frame, not rounded to 8, and writes H.265 Main 10 (MP4), ProRes 4444 (MOV), VP9 profile 2 (WebM) or 16-bit PNG; the browser's export offers 10 bits for MP4 and WebM when this browser encodes them (asked to WebCodecs first: H.265 Main 10, AV1 or VP9 profile 2) and says when it cannot. Gradients made by the effects and the finish keep their levels; exports stay SDR.
- **Long jobs that resume**: the video jobs run in chunks saved as they finish, say how long is left, can be cancelled, and pick up where they stopped. Two of them: `upscale` makes a picture or a video twice its size (Lanczos, lightly sharpened), and `interpolate` makes up the frames between a video's frames (2 or 4 times as many, along their motion), saved as a copy linked to it (`interpolated`, a new optional field of an asset): a layer slowed under 1× plays the copy and slows smoothly instead of repeating frames.
- **Smoother skin, when asked**: the `skin` tool finds the faces of a video (MediaPipe's Face Landmarker, in the browser) and saves the skin of each as a mask video, eyes, brows and mouth left out; `fx.smooth`, a surface blur that keeps edges, softens the video through it, lightly by default. The landmarker joins the models of the settings (Faces).
- **Hesitations cut**: the `fillers` tool finds the "uh"s and "um"s a transcript leaves out (where a voice detector, Silero VAD, hears speech and no word is written; the ones a transcription model wrote too) and cuts them as the silences are cut, the transcript of the edit remapped and the captions reconnected. Each cut is listed with its moment, and `restore` puts any back by its number. Silero VAD joins the models of the settings (Where someone speaks).
- **A clean voice**: the `denoise` tool takes the background noise out of a voice (RNNoise, in WebAssembly, loaded when first used, faster than real time) and saves the cleaned voice beside the original, which stays (`clean`, a new optional field of an asset); the layer plays the two mixed by its Clean voice amount, 0.85 by default so the voice keeps its life. The check warns about a voice whose noise floor is high, and the assistant cleans such a voice before captions and sound design.
- **Parallel export**: `tramme render` cuts the film into segments rendered at once by several pages of the same Chrome (as many as the machine's cores and memory allow, `--pages n` to choose, `--pages 1` for one after the other), each encoding its own, then joins them without encoding again; the sound is mixed once over the whole film. The frames are the ones a single page draws, simulations included.
- **Components in the editor**: an instance shows its component's parameters in the inspector like a node's properties (animatable, linkable), its overrides marked and removable, a button to open its component and one to detach it; any property of a composition becomes a parameter from its menu (Expose as a parameter), and the composition lists its parameters and how many instances use it. Components are marked in the compositions list, and kept in the library shared by the projects (`component-keep`, `component-use`).
- **The assistant writes things once**: `define_component` turns layers into a component and an instance in their place, `instantiate` places one or a whole distribution of it, `detach` turns an instance back into layers (the / menu has them too). The assistant's guide asks for a component as soon as something repeats, and a new check, Repeated elements, points at layers written several times nearly the same. The section titles, keywords, lower thirds and end cards are placed as instances of a component of their look, their words as parameters: two keywords share one component.
- **The time field**: the `timeField` modifier reads an animation later for some elements than for others, by rank (the old Offset, which it now runs), along a direction, by distance to a point or a layer, or by noise that can drift: a wave, a ripple, a liquid time, from one animation. A distribution takes one too (`time`), so a hundred instances become one wave. A modifier's amount or seed can be keyframed and slides.
- **Distributions**: a comp layer makes N instances of its component at once (`distribute`), on a grid, a line, a circle, along a path or on another layer's points, as many as asked or one per row of a JSON asset; each parameter is given by a rule (a value, an expression of `index`, `count` and `row`, the palette in order or at random). Each instance has its rank, so the offsets and the noise of the modifiers keep working across instances. Expressions read `index` and `count` everywhere.
- **Components**: a composition that lists `params` (each with its type, the properties it lands on and a default) is a component, and every comp layer holding it an instance with its own values (`params`, fixed or animated) and its own changes to any property inside (`overrides`); changing the component changes every instance. An instance can be detached into plain layers that draw the same. **Styles** are sets of properties defined once at the document's top level and applied to any layer (`styles`); keyframed, a style is an animation played from where it is used. New optional fields of the document.
- **A Models section in the settings**: the model each local tool runs (the cut-out of a person or of any subject, detection, depth, transcription) is named with its size and licence, and another can be chosen for this computer, among models whose input and output Tramme knows; detection, depth and transcription also take any model of the hub made for their pipeline, tried on a blank input before it is kept. A model whose licence forbids commercial work says so.
- **The models in this browser, by hand**: the Models section says whether each model is downloaded and how much it weighs, downloads it ahead of its tool with its progress, deletes it, and lists the models no tool uses any more with the space they take.
- **Transcription without a server or a companion**: the desktop app and the personal deployment transcribe in the browser itself when neither the server nor the local companion can, with the model chosen in the settings; the companion runs that model too (`TRAMME_WHISPER_MODEL` still overrides it on its machine).
- **An asset made by a model says which**: the cut-out's masks, the pictures cut out, the depth maps, the people found, the tracks and the transcripts keep the models and versions that made them (`madeBy`, a new optional field of the document); another model chosen later never changes them.
- **Voice effects**: a sound or video layer's voice can be pitched (in semitones, its length kept), or sound like a robot, a telephone, a radio, a megaphone or an echo, the same in the preview and the exports; with Keep the pitch, a voice at another speed or on a speed curve keeps its pitch.
- **Proxies**: the `proxy` tool encodes a lighter copy of a video (540 lines) that the preview plays instead, so a 4K or high frame rate file plays smoothly while editing; the exports keep the original. Importing a video this computer decodes too slowly offers one (measured on a second of it). Settings → Preview → Play proxies.
- **Stabilisation**: the `stabilize` tool takes the camera's shake out of a video, smoothed or locked still, shot by shot, and the video layer zooms just enough for the whole clip to keep its edges out.
- **Scopes**: a waveform and a vectorscope of the frame show beside the picture while a layer with a grading effect (LUT, curves, wheels, colour correction, key) is selected.
- **Colour wheels**: `fx.wheels` pushes the shadows, the mid tones and the highlights toward a colour (lift, gamma, gain, each a wheel to drag in the inspector) and sets their levels.
- **Curves**: `fx.curves` tones a layer by a master curve and one each for red, green and blue, edited as points on a square in the inspector.
- **LUTs**: a `.cube` file imported into the project (a `lut` asset) grades a layer through `fx.lut`, at a strength: a look, or the conversion of log footage.
- **Masks on any layer**: drawn outlines that cut a layer, each added, subtracted or intersected with the ones before it, with its feather, expansion, opacity and invert, its path animatable like a shape's (`masks`, a new optional field of a layer); a Masks section in the inspector; their outlines are edited in the viewport with the path tools (drag a vertex or a tangent, Alt+click adds a vertex, Delete removes it), and a mask can follow a tracked object (Follow a track). Expressions read a track at another time too: `track(layer, name, at)`.
- **Chroma key**: `fx.key` makes a green or blue screen transparent, in light and in shade alike, with its tolerance, its softness, the spill taken out of the edges, a choke, and a matte view to judge it. Its colour, like every colour of the inspector, can be picked in the viewport with a dropper (the effect off while picking).
- **Transitions between shots**: `fx.transition` on the incoming shot mixes it with the outgoing one over their overlap: crossfade, dip to a colour, push, slide, zoom, whip pan (both blurred along their move), a luma wipe from a grey picture, and a glitch. Their sounds cross with the picture (equal power), unless the transition says otherwise. In the timeline, a clip's menu puts a transition on the cut before it, the two shots overlapping from their files' handles (or saying how much is missing), and its band drags its length. The assistant reaches for one kind per film, cuts by default. GL effects also read `uLayerTime`, the time since their layer's in point.
- **Speed curves**: the speed of a video or sound layer can be animated; the file plays at the integral of the speed, so a shot slows into its moment and speeds out of it, the same in the preview and the exports. 0 freezes the frame, a negative speed plays backwards. Tracks, callouts and the sounds placed on what is seen follow the ramp, and captions follow the layer whose speech they show (`follow`). The sound follows the curve too, its pitch with the speed. The curves editor has four speed presets for the selected video or sound: ramp in, ramp out, hero moment and bullet.
- **A file already in the project is not imported twice**: importing it again from the assets panel, attaching it to a message for the assistant, or finding it twice among the sources of a new project gives back the asset that has it, and nothing is written again. Each imported asset keeps a hash of its content (`hash`, a new optional field of the document).
- **An export says where a frame's time goes**: next to the `ms/frame`, `tramme render` prints the time per frame spent evaluating the document, drawing it, waiting for video frames, reading the pixels back from the GPU and waiting for the encoder, so a slow export tells whether the JavaScript, the GPU, the decoding or the transfer holds it back.
- **Load cases to measure the renderer**: `tramme load` renders documents that each push one part of a frame (400 animated layers, blurs and effects with motion blur, a 4K source video, simulations) and prints, per case, the time per frame, its parts and what holds it back; `--json` keeps the figures and `--before` compares a later run with them, figure by figure.

- Perception tools: `beats` analyses music (tempo, beats, bars, sections, loudness per band), `shots` finds the cuts of a video, `subjects` finds the people in a video or an image (a small model run in the browser), `palette` turns the colours of a picture into tokens.
- Following the music: the `react` modifier and `audio()` in expressions make any property move with the beats, bars, hits or loudness of an analysed sound.
- Check `text-over-subject`: warns when text covers a face, once the people are known.
- Text behind the subject: the `cutout` tool (assistant and `/cutout`) cuts the person, or the main subject, out of a video layer with a small model run in the browser, saves the matte as a mask video timed like the file, and adds the same footage through that mask above the video. Titles placed between the two pass behind the person, in the preview and in every export.
- GPU effects: plugins write layer and finishing effects in GLSL; new built-in effects `fx.matte` (track matte from another layer), `fx.displace` (displacement map), `look.chromatic` (chromatic aberration), `look.grade` (colour grade in linear light). New property type `layer`.
- In the preview, a layer or effect that fails is drawn as a red frame and named in the error bar instead of blanking the whole picture.
- 2.5D camera: a composition can have a camera (pan, zoom, perspective, focus, depth blur) and layers a depth, for parallax and depth of field.
- Viewport handles declared by nodes (the rectangle's corner radius, plugin nodes' own points).
- Presets in the add menu: neon title, frosted card, light leak, spotlight; plugins add their own `presets`.
- Plugin nodes can say how they export to SVG and Lottie (`export.svg`, `export.lottie`).
- Plugin manifest (`meta` with the plugin API version, checked at load) and the `@tramme/plugin` package (types, `definePlugin()`).
- Plugin library shared by the projects: keep a project's plugin there and use it in another (`/library-add`, `/library-use`).
- Simulations: a node can carry a state from frame to frame (`simulate`), stepped deterministically with checkpoints; nodes read other layers with `host.layer()`. New `follow` node: a dot following a layer on a spring, with a trail.
- The assistant knows when each built-in node, effect and modifier fits (notes in `list_nodes`).
- Example project "Night sky": a project plugin with a node, a tool and a workflow.
- Plugins roadmap: `docs/plugins-roadmap.md`.
- Sound roadmap: `docs/sound-roadmap.md` (sound effects, ambiences and music made by the assistant in the editor).
- Sound library: 421 recorded sounds (Kenney's packs, CC0: impacts, interface, digital, jingles, sci-fi) measured and described in `sounds/catalog.json`, and 13 sounds written as code (whoosh, riser, sub boom, cinematic impact, glitch, sparkle…); your own sounds in a library shared by the projects (`/api/sounds`).
- Sound tools for the assistant and the `/` menu: `sfx` searches the library and places a sound on the moments the document names (entrances, exits, markers, cuts, beats, bars), its hit on the frame, its variants alternating; `synth` makes a sound from Web Audio code and answers with its waveform and measures; `duck` lowers music under a voice; `sound-keep` keeps a sound in the library; `generate-sound` has a provider make a sound effect, a music bed (ElevenLabs) or a voice-over (ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Gemini). The `/sound-design` workflow, and the assistant's guide for sound.
- The `generate-image` tool (assistant and `/generate-image`): a provider (OpenAI, Gemini, Z.AI) draws a picture the project lacks — a background a title reads over, a texture, an illustration, a prop — from its prompt, in the proportions of the composition; the file and what made it (`assets/images/<name>.image.json`: prompt, provider, model, proportions) join the project, the picture is proposed as an image layer (filling the frame or whole, over the whole composition or from a time, in the background on demand), and the tool answers with the picture so the assistant judges what was made. The keys stay in the Worker (`/api/generate-image`, `/api/config` lists the providers that draw); nothing is sent without the user's yes (the same setting as the paid sounds). The assistant's guide says when to have a picture made and why words never go in one (text layers say them).
- Generation roadmap: `docs/generation-roadmap.md` (pictures made by a provider, then takes, image to image, moving pictures, and what checks read in a picture).
- Motion figures: what the numbers of an animated property say (`packages/core/src/motion.ts`), sampled from the same evaluation the render uses — how far it travels, peak speed and when, evenness of the speed (1 is the linear tell), direction changes, how far it overshoots its end value, and when it settles. The `motion` tool answers with the figures of the span's biggest travels beside its strip of frames, so the assistant judges a movement with its numbers, not only the spacing. New checks that read them: `linear-travel` (a position or scale at constant speed for 0.3 s or more: ease it; a spring, a wiggle or any turn stays silent), `spring-settle` (a spring still moving when its layer leaves: more damping or more time; or overshooting its mark by more than 30%: one bounce is lively, more is nervous) and `twin-motion` (two layers whose positions follow the same series: offset, stagger or vary one). Motion roadmap: `docs/motion-roadmap.md`.
- Motion taste: the craft's rules, distilled from the public references of the field (the Animator's Survival Kit, Disney's twelve principles, the Material, Carbon and Apple motion systems, kinetic typography) and encoded where the engine acts on them. A "Motion" section in the assistant's guide: space before time (an even speed is the missing ease), entrances of 200 to 400 ms eased out and exits faster, one moving subject at a time with an uneven stagger, anticipation, arcs on the long travels, settles instead of hard stops, springs for what lands and curves for what intends, the reading time of text, and curves defined once as ease tokens (out, in, standard, emphasized, also named in `docs/document.md`). Three checks that read the figures: `abrupt-entrance` (the first move after a layer appears runs at full speed: ease it in), `straight-travel` (a big diagonal travel that keeps to its straight line: bend the move) and `uniform-stagger` (a group entering with even offsets: vary the gaps). The `/polish` workflow runs the whole loop on a composition: check and motion tools, the fixes the guide asks for, check again. The `bar-chart` recipe's fades now ease with the kit's curve. Taste roadmap and sources: `docs/taste-roadmap.md`.
- Motion sense everywhere the model looks: the notes per node family carry what each thing's movement should be (text moves as a block or word by word, a counter rolls with an ease out, a bar draws along its x, a path draws itself over 0.4 to 0.8 s, a callout follows without lag, captions pop on the spoken word, a group staggers its children, footage drifts or zooms slowly and never both); the style kits expose the four curves as tokens, `standard` and `emphasized` beside `enter` and `exit`, flavored per kit; and the `motion` tool reads the pixels of its frames (optical flow by block matching between two stills): it says when the whole frame moves and how fast in px/s, and points at the blocks that move against the rest — a background that should hold, something crossing the move.
- Themes: the interface colours become a list of themes, one palette chosen in the settings and kept in the browser: Studio (the former look), Obsidian (near-black, the chrome recedes into the room), Carbon (pure graphite, no blue in it), Dusk (a warm dark for the long sessions), Paper (the former light grey), and System (follows the computer). The meaning of colours stays across themes (selection, keyframes, expressions, links); the picker shows three swatches per theme, and an old preference migrates.
- Display modes: the same interface, laid out differently (top bar's expand button, or the settings). The editor stays the default; Cinema gives the film the whole window — the panels become floating glass cards summoned from the edges (layers, properties, the transport and the timeline as a bottom drawer); Conversation makes the assistant thread the main column, with the film beside it and the layers one edge away. A display change re-measures the stage.
- The `shorts` tool (assistant and `/shorts`): a long video as shorts, in two calls. First the candidates: the transcript grouped on pauses and sentence ends into passages that stand alone (15 to 45 s by default), each with its times and what is said. Then a plan of passages built into one composition per short: the video filling the asked format (vertical 1080×1920, square, horizontal) and starting at the passage's `start`, the transcript remapped to the short, saved as its own asset and captioned (the `captions` template), the title with its bar (the `title` template). Each short is an ordinary composition: editable, checked and exported like any other. The frame follows the speaker: `focus` on the video layer (the point of the picture kept at the centre of the frame, as the image layer had; animated, it follows the subject in a cropped frame), which the build animates across each short from the subjects analysis of the video when there is one — the biggest person of each analysed frame, held when nobody is found, drifts under 4 % of the picture ignored. The `/shorts` workflow, and the assistant's guide for shorts (a short stands alone, hooks in its first sentence, ends on a point). `transcriptIdOf` moved to `@tramme/core` with the rest of the transcript domain. Shorts roadmap: `docs/shorts-roadmap.md`.
- Sound layers: volume animatable (fades, ducking), `fadeIn`, `fadeOut`, `lowCut`, `highCut`, `reverb`, and `rate` (speed and pitch) on audio layers; the same but `rate` on the sound of videos.
- The `check` tool reads the mix: clipping, a mix far too loud or quiet, two sounds starting together.
- Sound settings (Settings, Sound): the preview's volume and mute (the exports keep the mix), the level of effects and music beds the tools place, how much a music drops under a voice, varying repeated sounds, asking before a paid sound (a dialog; nothing is sent to the provider without a yes), the provider and voice for voice-overs, and your sound library (listen, delete).
- Guided tour "Give the video its sound" (help menu): the library, asking the assistant, the `/` sound tools, sound layers and their properties, checking the mix, exporting with the sound.
- Assistant effort levels, chosen for each model next to the model menu and in the settings: Claude Opus and Sonnet (low to max, `high` by default, where Opus 5.5 alone would run at `medium`), and the reasoning models of the other providers (OpenAI o-series and GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 and later, OpenRouter models that reason, gpt-oss on a local server: low, medium, high, or the model's own default). A model that refuses its level runs at its own.
- The assistant tells you when it is done (Settings, Assistant): a notification from the system when the editor is in the background, a message in the editor after a long task.
- Automatic application of the assistant's changes (Settings, Assistant), turned on after a confirmation: a proposal is applied as soon as the turn is over, still checked first, shown in the conversation and undone with Ctrl+Z.
- Event lists: a JSON asset of timed events (`{ id, t, label, detail?, values, set? }`) that keep running totals: changes that add up (money in any currency, laughs, points, kilometres) or values an event sets (a weight, a temperature). Written by the `events` tool (assistant and `/events`) from lines such as `3.1 Petrol cash -18 | full tank` or `9 Weigh-in weight =72.5`, and totals written as they should look: `Cash: £23.67`, `0,00 €`, `Days: 0 day|0 days`, `Time: 0:00`. Expressions read it with `events()` (`.total()`, `.text()`, `.since()`, `.pulse()`, `.last()`…). `event-counter` puts the running totals in a corner, counters that roll at each change; `event-tags` a tag at each event (`PETROL −£18.00`, or the total it leaves; new `events.tag` node); `event-receipt` every event on a receipt typed line by line, then its totals (new `events.receipt` node). Every one of these layers reads the list as it renders: save the list again and they all follow. Example project "Road trip".
- Counters: `direction`, `down` rolls the digits back, for a value that goes down.
- Tracked callouts: the `track` tool (assistant and `/track`) follows something filmed through a video, framed by a rectangle drawn over it, a region of the picture or its name (person, car, dog… found by the detector), and saves where it is over time (`track-<video>-<name>`); a cut ends the track. The `callout` tool puts brackets and a label on it (new `callout` node) that stay on it, even when the video layer is moved or retimed. Expressions read where it is with `track(layer, name?)`.

### Changed

- The project format (still `tramme-project/1`) admits PDF documents under `assets/`. Every project of 0.1.0 opens as before; a project holding a PDF (made from sources, or with a document attached to the assistant) does not open in 0.1.0.
- Tool contexts gain `files()` and `fileUrl(path)`: plugin tools may use them; a host that builds tool contexts itself must provide them.
- Switched project license from MIT to Apache-2.0.
- Provider keys are connected in the editor, under Settings, Providers: Connect, Change key, Disconnect for Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter, Z.AI and ElevenLabs. The Worker keeps them in R2 and never sends them back; Cloudflare secrets still work, a key connected in the settings comes first.
- Custom providers: any service of the OpenAI chat format (DeepSeek, Groq, Mistral…) added by name, base URL and key; its models show in the model menu (`custom:<id>:model`).
- Settings in sections: a menu on the left (Editor: Appearance, Preview, Guided tours; Assistant: Model, Providers, Behavior, Connection) with a search that finds a setting whatever its section, the chosen section on the right; on a phone, the sections as tabs.
- The assistant's conversation with Claude is append-only: nothing already sent is rewritten, so the prompt cache stays warm and Claude's thinking stays valid. Old tool results are cleared by the API (context editing) once the conversation grows; the system prompt is cached for an hour.
- Lighter tool answers: `list_nodes` gives an index (properties as `name:type=default`, notes, tool inputs) and the full entries on demand (`types`), about 60% fewer tokens; `get_document` reads one part with `path`; `read_file` reads long files in parts (`offset`).
- Longer answers (64k tokens) on the server path; a request Claude declines goes to another model on the server (`fallbacks`), and the user is told when it is declined anyway.
- One mixer for the preview, the browser exports and the command line (`@tramme/render`): the command line mixes in its page and hands ffmpeg the finished track, instead of its own ffmpeg filter graphs.

### Fixed

- Expressions that read an analysis (`audio()`) or an event list (`events()`) now have their values in the inspector, the curves, the viewport's boxes and the assistant's `evaluate`, instead of neutral ones.
- On phones, the assistant's message box no longer slips under the tab bar when the conversation is long.
- Counters: separators and units (`,` `%` `€`) take their own width instead of a digit's.
- MP4 export: the H.264 configuration (avcC) is written from the stream itself. The one some encoders give (Media Foundation under Windows) was malformed, so Windows, QuickTime and phones refused the file ("format not supported") while Chrome and VLC played it.
- Assistant, server path: stopping during several tool calls no longer breaks the next message; a tool call whose input is invalid or cut off is no longer run with an empty input (the model is told and calls again); inputs are checked against each tool's schema on every path.
- Assistant, server path: a request part the API refuses (thinking display, context editing, effort…) is dropped on its own instead of turning off the thinking summary; retries wait as long as the server asks (`retry-after`).
- Assistant: a server that does not answer, or an answer whose stream breaks before anything was shown, is tried again by itself (twice) instead of ending the turn.
- 2.5D camera: a nested composition is drawn through its own camera (parallax and depth of field); it was ignored everywhere but in the root composition.
- Check `stillness`: a nested composition counts as playing, as a video does, instead of making its parent look frozen; its own still stretches are found when it is checked.
- Counters: digits share one cell again, as wide as the widest digit (the test looked for the letter "d", so each digit took its own width and the number shifted as it rolled); the `stat` and `bar-chart` recipes measure them the same way.
- `tramme pack` and `tramme unpack` check a project with its own plugins loaded, as the editor's import does; a project using plugin nodes (the night-sky example) was refused with "unknown node type".

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-02

First public release.

### Engine

- JSON document as the source of truth: nested compositions, layers, groups, assets, design tokens, markers and sound. Format `tramme/1`, described in `docs/document.md`, with a JSON schema in `schema/tramme-1.schema.json`.
- Deterministic rendering: every frame is a pure function of time.
- Every property can be static, keyframed, an expression or a link, with an optional modifier stack (wiggle, loop, spring, offset, variation, smoothing).
- Open vocabulary: each layer type is a node that declares its property schema. The inspector and the validator are generated from it.
- Native nodes: shapes, path, image, text, counter, group, composition, particles, GLSL shader, code, sound, image sequence, video, captions.
- Layer effects: blur, shadow, glow, color, tint.
- Canvas2D layer renderer and WebGL2 compositor (motion blur, linear color, finishing).
- Project plugins: projects can add their own nodes, effects and modifiers as JS modules.

### Projects

- Project format `tramme-project/1` (manifest, allowed paths, checks), exchanged as `.tramme` archives. Projects created under the former names ("trame", "emotion") are still read.

### Editor

- Projects home: recent projects with thumbnails, new project (formats, frame rate, duration), project from a video, opening a `.tramme` file or a Lottie animation, examples.
- Viewport with zoom, pan, safe zones, grid, and direct manipulation (move, scale, rotate).
- Timeline (layer bars, keyframes, markers), Bézier curve editor, inspector generated from the schemas.
- Undo and redo for every change, autosave with conflict detection across tabs.
- Hand-drawn animation: image sequences held on twos or driven by an exposure sheet.
- Video layers decoded frame by frame with WebCodecs, speech transcription to timed captions.
- In-browser export: MP4 (H.264), WebM (VP9 with alpha), GIF, PNG sequence, Lottie, SVG still, WAV.
- Guided tours, settings (theme, preview quality), English and French interface.
- Mobile and touch support: tabbed panels under 760 px, a compact header with a "more" menu, pinch to zoom and two-finger pan in the viewport, larger touch targets, installable as a web app.

### Assistant

- AI assistant that edits the document through the same operations as the editor: proposals are previewed, then applied or rejected, and can be undone.
- Claude through a local companion (`tramme agent`, Claude Agent SDK) or through the server with an API key.
- Other providers: OpenAI, Gemini and OpenRouter through the server, local models (Ollama, LM Studio) from the browser.
- Image, sound and video attachments; video dressing (cuts, captions, templates).

### Command line

- `tramme` CLI: validate, still frames, offline rendering (MP4, MOV ProRes 4444, WebM with alpha, GIF, PNG, WAV) with headless Chrome and ffmpeg, Lottie and SVG export, Lottie import, pack and unpack, comparisons and benchmarks.

### Deployment

- Cloudflare Worker serving the editor and the API, projects stored in R2, access protected by Cloudflare Access.
